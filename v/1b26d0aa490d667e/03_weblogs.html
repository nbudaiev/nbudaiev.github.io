The pipeline and its weblog

What the pipeline is, which data one weblog covers, and where it sits in what you download.

Conceptthe CASA pipeline and its weblog

What are the pipeline and the weblog?

  • Recipe: a fixed sequence of stages that calibrate the raw data, then image them. One recipe for ALMA interferometry (12-m and 7-m), others for total power and the VLA.
  • Heuristics: determine which steps are needed and their parameters, then run the core CASA tasks (flagdata, gaincal, tclean, …).
  • Weblog: one HTML page per stage with plots, tables, images and a QA score. Written as the pipeline runs. Holds most QA2 diagnostics.

QA2

ALMA staff check every weblog and add flags, recalibrate or reimage as needed to reach the PI's requested rms and beam. Pipeline scores are conservative (set for automatic delivery). The QA2 report in qa/ explains yellow and red scores.

EB: one execution of the scheduling block. One weblog includes all EBs. Targets and calibrators are imaged with all EBs combined, the check source per EB.

Early-cycle data, such as the Cycle 0 TW Hya set of Parts 4 and 5, were calibrated manually, with no weblog.

In qa/: tar -xzf *.weblog.tgz, then open pipeline-*/html/index.html (Firefox works best). Delivered only for pipeline-processed data. Read it and the QA2 report before you download raw data.

From script/: casa --pipeline -c scriptForPI.py with the version named in the QA2 report and weblog. It applies the delivered calibration to the raw data.

Why robust may not be 0.5

hifa_imageprecheck picks robust 0, 0.5, 1 or 2 to match the PI's requested beam (0.5 if it can). hif_checkproductsize may bin channels (nbins) to keep cubes small. Start from these values, then adjust (Part 4).

Flagging bad data

Most problems on the Check pages are bad data (an antenna, a spw, a time range). Add one line each to uid*flagtemplate.txt, read by hifa_flagdata when you rerun. Flag what the calibrators show is bad. Be conservative with target-only outliers, which may be real emission.

Ask the Helpdesk

For doubts about the flux scale (flux.csv) or antenna positions (antennapos.csv), or a problem you cannot trace to bad data.

Fixed by the observationthe atmosphere · WVR, gaincal

Phase fluctuations from water vapour

TryExcellent → poor weather. The screen and the phases grow (fixed colour scale). Band 3 → Band 9 gives 7× more phase for the same path. Phase noise grows roughly as b5/6 on short baselines, more slowly beyond ~1 km, and levels off at a few km. With time the screen moves over the array.

Fixed by the observationhow ALMA observes

Observing sequence: calibrator and target scans

1 · A priori

WVR, Tsys and antenna-position tables. No calibrator needed.

2 · Bandpass

Bright quasar, once. Frequency response of every antenna.

3 · Gains

Phase calibrator every few minutes. Interpolate to the target.

4 · Flux scale

Calibrator of known flux. Others are scaled to it.

The observatory sets this schedule (which calibrators, how long, how often). The check source is a second quasar imaged with the target's calibration. If it is point-like and at the right position, phase transfer worked.

Conceptthe measurement equation

Antenna-based gains: N antennas, N(N−1)/2 baselines

Vijobs = BiBj* GiGj* Vijtrue + noise

B(ν): bandpass, G(t): complex gain of each antenna, both measured on calibrators.

TryTurn the phase of antenna 3. Every baseline with antenna 3 changes. The closure phase of each triangle stays the same. This is why calibration (and self-calibration) can solve for antenna gains.

Fixed by the observationphase referencing · cycle time, separation

Phase referencing: cycle time and separation

TryShorten the cycle from 6 to 1 min. The residual drops from 26° to 15° and the peak rises from 86% to 95%, with less time on target. Beyond ~3 min the calibrator no longer follows the atmosphere, so longer cycles change little. A more distant calibrator gives larger direction-dependent errors. The observatory set all three (Band 7 here).

Fixed by the observationflux calibrators

Flux scale: quasars and resolved planets

TryTitan, C-2 → C-6. The baselines with less than half its flux go from 2% to 91%. Uranus (3.6″) is resolved even in C-2 (93%). setjy models the disk. The pipeline limits uvrange for it. Quasars are points but vary, so ALMA monitors them for its catalogue. Fixed x axis.

From the weblog

Field names, refant and spwmap are in the weblog and in casa_commands.log. Use the same ones.

Your choicebandpass · solint, combine

Choosing bandpass parameters

TryOne scan, solint='inf'. The bright target's spectrum is noisier than thermal (readout). combine='scan' uses both scans. 'inf,4ch' averages 4 channels and lowers the noise. At 'inf,16ch' the blue line misses the steep band edges. The pipeline averages until S/N ≈ 50 (solint on the hifa_bandpass page).

Your choicegaincal · solint, gaintype, combine, minsnr

Choosing gaincal parameters

TryAt 'int' solutions scatter, low-S/N ones most (red: below minsnr, flagged). 'inf' is less noisy but flat per scan (zoom). Lowest error: 'int' or '18s' with gaintype='T' (×1.4) and combine='spw' (×2, needs hifa_spwphaseup).

calwt, applymode

calwt updates weights. 'calflag' (default) flags data whose solutions are flagged. A spw with no solution at all passes uncalibrated unless you use 'calflagstrict'. 'calonly' ignores solution flags.

gaintable + gainfield

All tables are applied together in measurement-equation order. List position only pairs each table with its gainfield, interp and spwmap.

interp

In time: 'linear' between calibrator scans is phase referencing. 'nearest' copies the closest solution. After a comma, in frequency: 'linearflag' for the bandpass.

spwmap

Reuses solutions from one spw for others: the Tsys table (measured in TDM windows), or gains solved with combine='spw'.

Your choiceapplycal · interp, gainfield, spwmap

Applying solutions to the target

TryTable solved with combine='spw'. 'nearest': the gain jumps halfway between scans. gainfield = bandpass cal (one old solution, 20° away) smears the target. spwmap=[]: spws 1–3 find no solution and, with the default applymode='calflag', pass uncalibrated and unflagged. The target fades. 'calflagstrict' would flag them.

Leave at defaultleave it to the pipeline

Standard calibration sequence

ms0, ms = 'uid.ms', 'uid.cal.ms'                          # the pipeline's MS (working/), your calibrated copy
ref, bpc, phc = 'DV17,DA45,DA41', 'J1058+0133', 'J1037-2934'  # refants (hif_refant), bandpass/flux cal, phase cal
sci, tsysmap = '17,19,21,23', TSYSMAP                    # science spws, Tsys spw map: copy both from casa_commands.log
# 1 · a-priori tables from the pipeline: Tsys (measured on each field), WVR, antenna positions; keep the science spws
for f in [bpc, phc, 'TARGET']:
    applycal(vis=ms0, field=f, spw=sci, gaintable=['uid.tsys', 'uid.wvr', 'uid.antpos'], gainfield=[f, '', ''],
             interp='linear,linear', spwmap=[tsysmap, [], []], calwt=True, flagbackup=False)
split(vis=ms0, outputvis=ms, spw=sci, datacolumn='corrected')                    # the science spws are now 0–3
# 2 · bandpass
setjy(vis=ms, field=bpc, standard='manual', fluxdensity=[1.2, 0, 0, 0], spix=-0.6, reffreq='233GHz')  # catalogue
gaincal(vis=ms, caltable='bp.ph', field=bpc, solint='int', calmode='p', refant=ref)                # phase-up
bandpass(vis=ms, caltable='uid.bp', field=bpc, solint='inf', combine='scan', refant=ref,
         solnorm=True, gaintable=['bp.ph'])                                  # solnorm as the pipeline (CASA default False)
# 3 · gains and flux scale
gaincal(vis=ms, caltable='uid.phase_int', field=bpc + ',' + phc, solint='int', calmode='p', refant=ref,
        gaintable=['uid.bp'])
gaincal(vis=ms, caltable='uid.amp_inf', field=bpc + ',' + phc, solint='inf', calmode='ap', refant=ref,
        gaintable=['uid.bp', 'uid.phase_int'])
fluxscale(vis=ms, caltable='uid.amp_inf', fluxtable='uid.flux_inf', reference=bpc, transfer=phc)
gaincal(vis=ms, caltable='uid.phase_inf', field=phc, solint='inf', calmode='p', refant=ref, gaintable=['uid.bp'])
# 4 · apply to the target: bandpass, then the phase cal's solutions interpolated in time
applycal(vis=ms, field='TARGET', gaintable=['uid.bp', 'uid.phase_inf', 'uid.flux_inf'], gainfield=['', phc, phc],
         interp=['linear,linearflag', 'linear', 'linear'], calwt=True)

Weak phase calibrator: first gaintype='T' or a longer solint, then combine='spw' with spwmap=[0,0,0,0] in applycal, after hifa_spwphaseup removed the spw offsets. Plot every table (Check slides) before the next step.

Checkquestions

Four weblog questions

  1. Every antenna's Tsys has the same bump at one frequency. Should you flag it?
    AnswerNo. A bump on every antenna and field at one frequency is an atmospheric line, handled by the Tsys calibration. A bump on one field only is line emission from that source. Flag those Tsys channels.
  2. In hifa_timegaincal one antenna's phase climbs a steady 8° per minute and wraps round. Weather?
    AnswerUnlikely. The atmosphere does not drift steadily for an hour. Suspect a wrong antenna position. Check that antenna in hif_applycal. Flag it, or ask the Helpdesk for corrected positions (antennapos.csv).
  3. The check source is 150 mas off with 60% of its flux. The phase calibrator looks fine. What does that say about the target?
    AnswerPhase transfer to nearby sources was poor (atmosphere, separation, cycle time: fixed by the observation), so the target is probably smeared too. If it is bright, self-calibration (Part 6) can help.
  4. hif_makeimages: continuum rms 3.1× the expected value. Will cleaning deeper fix it?
    AnswerNot if calibration errors cause the noise. Look upstream: phase rms (hifa_spwphaseup, hifa_timegaincal), bad data left in hif_applycal, the check source. Check the mask too. Self-calibration may help a bright target.

Checkquestions

Four calibration questions

  1. How can 43 antennas be calibrated from 903 baselines, each with its own errors?
    AnswerThe errors are antenna-based, so 43 complex gains explain 903 baselines. The system is over-determined (closure quantities are free of antenna gains).
  2. Your Band 9 long-baseline data lose half the flux of the check source. Which calibration parameter fixes that?
    AnswerNone. The same water-vapour path gives 7× more phase at Band 9 than at Band 3, and the cycle time was fixed by the observation. Phase noise faster than the cycle cannot be interpolated. Only self-calibration on a bright target (Part 6) can help.
  3. The weblog's bandpass solutions are noisy, and your target is bright continuum with a faint line. What can you change?
    AnswerNot the calibrator. Average channels in the solution (solint='inf,4ch'), and use every bandpass scan (combine='scan'). Do not average so much that the band edges are smoothed away. The pipeline averages until S/N ≈ 50 (hifa_bandpass). Average more only if your bright target needs it.
  4. You solved the phases with combine='spw' and ran applycal without spwmap. Spws 1–3 of the target look smeared, and nothing was flagged. Why?
    AnswerThe table only holds solutions labelled with the first spw (0). With the default applymode='calflag', spws without solutions pass through uncalibrated and unflagged (only 'calflagstrict' flags them). Set spwmap=[0,0,0,0].
