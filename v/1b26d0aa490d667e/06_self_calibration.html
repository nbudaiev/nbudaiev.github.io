1

Why it works

Any source can be a calibrator if you have a good model of it.

Concepta quick review of calibration · tclean(savemodel)

Models: calibrator and target

TryThe calibrator is flat, with zero phase. Your target's amplitude falls with uv distance. With 'none' gaincal compares it to a flat 1 Jy point (an amplitude solution would scale the data up 20×). With 'modelcolumn' the model follows the data. Self-cal removes the remaining phase scatter. This disk is centred and symmetric, so its phases look the same either way. An off-centre or asymmetric source would also show phase structure against a point.

  • gaincal finds the antenna gains that make the data match a model.
  • A calibrator is bright, point-like and steady. Its model is one number, its flux.
  • tclean creates a model from the clean components. savemodel='modelcolumn' writes it for gaincal.
  • Clean only real emission. Fake sources in the model can appear in the data, more so with few antennas (7-m array).
  • Not circular: 903 baselines constrain 43 antenna gains, so a rough model is enough.

Concepterrors left after phase referencing

Why self-calibration?

  • Time: the calibrator brackets the target. In between, the atmosphere changes.
  • Direction: the calibrator is in another direction (different air, antenna position errors).
  • Signal: the calibrator may be weaker than your target.

TryBetween calibrator scans (dots) the correction (dashed) is a straight line. The true phase varies. Even at the dots it is up to 25° off (Direction). Perfect calibration gives about 10% more peak and no artifacts.

How it works

How much signal one solution needs, then the loop: clean, solve, apply, repeat with a shorter solint.

CheckS/N per solution, before you start

S/N for self-calibration

Resolved target? Long baselines see less than the peak (first demo: about 15 of the 48 mJy beyond 500 kλ, 12 beyond 1000 kλ). Use that flux (amplitude vs uv distance). If most baselines see S/N < 3, self-cal fails.

S/Nsolint ≈ (peak S/N of the image) × √(tsolint / ton source) / √(Nant − 3)

TryPhase-only needs S/N ≥ 3 per solution (5 to be safe), amplitude ≥ 10 (a 10% gain error). Only solint, combine and gaintype move the dot. combine='spw' (×2 with 4 spws) assumes the source is the same across the band, gaintype='T' (×√2) that it is unpolarized. 'scan,spw' with 'inf' gives one solution per execution (last resort for a faint target).

TryRun 'inf' → '60s' → '30s' → 'int', then one 'ap' round with solint 'inf'. Artifacts fade and the rms goes down, with diminishing returns. "Phase err" is the true remaining error (known only in a simulation).

How it works

  1. Plan solints from long to short (inf_EB, inf, …, int), cleaning deeper at each step.
  2. Each step: image and model, gaincal, applycal, image again.
  3. Pass: S/N up (also near the source), rms up by less than 5%, beam area up by less than 5%. Fail: apply the last successful table and stop.

Output

  • *_targets.ms: DATA has the regular calibration, CORRECTED_DATA the self-calibrated data, applied to all spws.
  • Archive images both with and without self-cal.
  • If applied, image CORRECTED_DATA. To redo it, start from DATA. Failed or not tried: see next slide.

Checklimits of automated self-cal

When to go manual

Not supported

  • Line self-cal (a maser or CO line brighter than the continuum).
  • Archive data processed before Cycle 10 were not self-calibrated. Rerun the imaging pipeline with a current version (hif_selfcal, Cycle 3 and later), or do it by hand.
  • Full polarization with a target polarized by more than a few %.
  • Ephemeris targets (planets, comets) and sources that change during the observation.
  • Mosaics whose faint fields need solutions from bright ones (combine='field': use only fields with strong signal).

Possible improvements

  • Amplitude self-cal is off by default: amplitude_selfcal=True (uses solnorm=True).
  • Large phase errors put artifacts in the first model, which then end up in the data. Lower dividing_factor (default 15) for a shallower first clean.
  • Not tried, S/N too low: lower minsnr_to_proceed (default 3), at the risk of fitting noise.
  • Too faint on long baselines: those antennas stay uncalibrated, short baselines still improve.

Notes

  • Manual self-cal is rarely much better. Use it for the cases on the left, or to learn the process.
  • Improvement: ~10× in dynamic range for a bright source, little for a faint one.
  • Read more: the CASA guide First Look at Self Calibration (TW Hya, its output twhya_selfcal.ms is the start of the Part 5 follow-along), ALMA Memo 620 (Brogan et al. 2018), github.com/jjtobin/auto_selfcal.

A self-calibration recipe to copy

# 0. check: hif_selfcal has not done it, and peak S/N × √(t_solint / t_on source) / √(N_ant − 3) ≥ 3
# 1. a continuum copy of the target: flag the lines, average, unflag (detailed recipe: Backup)
#    4 spws here (25, 27, 29, 31; split makes them 0–3): one width, one spwmap entry each; refant: the pipeline's
#    several executions (one _targets.ms each): split each the same way, then concat(vis=[…], concatvis='sc.ms')
ms = 'uid___A002_X…_targets.ms'                                         # yours, from the pipeline
split(vis=ms, outputvis='sc.ms', field='<your target>', spw='25,27,29,31', datacolumn='data', width=[128,128,8,8])

# 2. model: clean conservatively and write MODEL_DATA
tclean(vis='sc.ms', imagename='sc_p0', imsize=640, cell='0.06arcsec',   # your Part 4 values
       specmode='mfs', weighting='briggs', robust=0.5, niter=100000, nsigma=5.0,
       usemask='auto-multithresh', savemodel='modelcolumn')

# 3. phase round: one solution per scan, all spws and both polarizations together
gaincal(vis='sc.ms', caltable='sc_p1.g', solint='inf', calmode='p', combine='spw',
        gaintype='T', refant='DA41,DA55', minsnr=3.0)
applycal(vis='sc.ms', gaintable=['sc_p1.g'], spwmap=[0, 0, 0, 0])

# 4. repeat 2-3: imagename 'sc_p1', 'sc_p2' …, nsigma 3.0 → 2.5, solint '60s' → '30s' → 'int';
#    apply only the newest table; stop when peak S/N gains < a few % or many solutions flag
plotms(vis='sc_p1.g', xaxis='time', yaxis='phase', iteraxis='antenna')    # every table: smooth in time, not noise

# 5. optional, once: amplitude on top of the last good phase table (here sc_p3.g)
gaincal(vis='sc.ms', caltable='sc_ap.g', solint='inf', calmode='ap', solnorm=True,
        combine='spw', gaintype='T', refant='DA41,DA55', minsnr=3.0,
        gaintable=['sc_p3.g'], spwmap=[0, 0, 0, 0])
applycal(vis='sc.ms', gaintable=['sc_p3.g', 'sc_ap.g'], spwmap=[[0, 0, 0, 0], [0, 0, 0, 0]])
#    keep it only if the image improves and the total flux changes by < a few %

# 6. lines (Part 5): the final tables on the full-resolution data of every _targets.ms, before uvcontsub
applycal(vis=ms, field='<your target>', gaintable=['sc_p3.g', 'sc_ap.g'], spwmap=[[0]*32, [0]*32])  # 25–31 → 0

Conceptoverview

Outline of self-calibration

Self-cal is ordinary gain calibration with the target's own model in place of a calibrator's.

  • The errors are antenna-based: 903 baselines constrain 43 gains, so an approximate model is enough.
  • Start with long solution intervals (per scan), then shorten as the model and the S/N allow.
  • Phase first. Amplitude only at the end, with a long solint, if it still helps.
  • Since Cycle 10 the ALMA pipeline runs continuum self-cal (hif_selfcal). Check its weblog page first.

Conceptscope

What self-calibration can and cannot fix

Fixes

  • Residual atmospheric phase errors on the target, down to seconds if S/N allows.
  • Slow antenna amplitude errors (amplitude self-cal).
  • Phase-transfer errors because the calibrator lies in another direction (e.g. antenna-position errors), as long as they are the same over your field.

Does not fix

  • Missing short spacings (add ACA or Total Power data). Bandpass errors and bad data (flag and recalibrate).
  • Errors that vary across the field (primary beam, pointing) for wide mosaics.
  • Targets with S/N < 3 per solution (self-cal fits the noise).

Costs

  • Phase self-cal ties the absolute position to the starting model. Measure positions on the phase-referenced image.
  • Amplitude self-cal can change the flux scale. Use solnorm=True, check fluxes before and after.

Prerequisites

  • A good initial image and mask. Clean deep enough that the model holds most of the flux.
  • Peak S/N of the starting image ≥ 3√(Nant−3) (≈ 20 with 43 antennas) for one solution per execution. About √(number of scans) times more for one per scan (≈ 45 here, 5 scans). See the S/N slide.

Your choicegaincal · solint

Choosing solint

TryOne fixed 3 mJy target. Short solints track fast variations but each solution is noisy (dashed: noise). Long ones are precise but average them out (dashed: tracking). Find the solint with the lowest total. For a brighter target the best solint is shorter.

Common issue 1: self-calibrating on noise

  

    

      

      

      
TryThe data hold only noise. With minsnr=0 every solution fits the noise to the 1 mJy model, and a "source" appears at the model position (between the marks). minsnr=3 rejects most solutions (left uncalibrated, applymode='calonly'), but the few that pass by chance leave a spurious source of about 6σ at 'int'.

    

    

      
      
    

  





  
Checkcalmode='ap'
Common issue 2: amplitude self-cal

  

    

      

      

      
TryMODEL_DATA holds only the core (cleaned too shallow). With 'p' both fluxes stay at 100%. With 'ap' the gains fit the data to the model and remove much of the halo. A longer solint cannot fix a wrong model. Clean deeply before any amplitude round. If the model still misses extended flux, leave the short baselines out of the 'ap' solve (uvrange='>…klambda', the cut read off amp vs uvdist in plotms).

    

    

      
      
    

  





  
Checkstopping · line data
When to stop, and line data

  

    
Stop when
  • Peak S/N improves by less than a few percent per round.
  • Solutions become noisy or many fall below minsnr.
  • The rms reaches the thermal noise. Remaining artifacts have another cause.
  • An amplitude round changes total flux by more than a few percent.

    
Watch for
  • Fluxes or structure that change with each round (model bias).
  • Large phase corrections on one antenna: likely bad data to flag.
  • New sources only at mask positions (common issue 1).

    
Line data
  • Derive solutions on the continuum (most S/N), apply them to the full-resolution MS with the same spws (or spwmap).
  • Apply self-cal before uvcontsub, then image the cube.
  • A bright line (maser, CO) can self-calibrate a cube itself.

    
Report
  • The final rounds, solints and the S/N gain in your paper or notes.
  • Keep the tables (valid for these executions only).

A self-calibration recipe: prepare, model, first round


# 0. check: S/N per solution (S/N slide) = peak S/N × √(t_solint / t_on source) / √(N_ant − 3)
#    ≥ 3 with t_solint = one scan: go on; only with all of t_on source: combine='scan,spw'; else stop

# 1. a continuum copy of the target: flag the lines, average, unflag
#    several executions (one _targets.ms each): split each the same way, then concat(vis=[…], concatvis='sc.ms')
#    and check listobs: spwmap needs one entry per spw of the result
ms = 'uid___A002_X…_targets.ms'                                             # from the pipeline (calibrated)
flagmanager(vis=ms, mode='save', versionname='before_cont')
flagdata(vis=ms, mode='manual', spw='25:600~800')                           # the line channels
split(vis=ms, outputvis='sc.ms', field='<your target>', datacolumn='data',  # regular calibration
      spw='25,27,29,31',                                                    # science spws (listobs); become 0–3

flagmanager(vis=ms, mode='restore', versionname='before_cont')              # lines back for Part 5

# 2. round 0: clean conservatively and write the model into sc.ms
tclean(vis='sc.ms', imagename='sc_p0', imsize=640, cell='0.06arcsec',       # your Part 4 values
       specmode='mfs', deconvolver='hogbom', weighting='briggs', robust=0.5,
       niter=100000, nsigma=5.0, usemask='auto-multithresh',                # nsigma stops it
       interactive=False, savemodel='modelcolumn')                          # gaincal needs MODEL_DATA

gaincal(vis='sc.ms', caltable='sc_p1.g', solint='inf', calmode='p',
        combine='spw', gaintype='T', refant='DA41,DA55', minsnr=3.0)
applycal(vis='sc.ms', gaintable=['sc_p1.g'], spwmap=[0, 0, 0, 0])           # one entry per spw

Checkdiscussion
Four questions

  
    
    
  1. Your continuum peak S/N is 15, with 43 antennas and 36 min on source (7-min scans). Can you self-calibrate with 60 s solutions?
    AnswerNo. S/N per solution ≈ 15 × √(60/2150) / √40 ≈ 0.4, per 7-min scan ≈ 1.1, and for the whole execution ≈ 2.4, all below minsnr = 3. The estimate assumes all spws and both polarizations, so combining cannot help. Keep the phase-referenced image, which is noise-limited.
    2. 
    
  2. After round 1 (solint='inf') the S/N went from 80 to 200. Round 2 ('30s') gives 205. Next step?
    AnswerStop the phase rounds (diminishing returns). Possibly one amplitude round with solint='inf' and solnorm=True. Compare fluxes before and after.
    3. 
    
  3. You solved with combine='spw'. After applycal the image hardly improves, and plotms shows the phases of spws 1–3 unchanged. What did you forget?
    Answerspwmap=[0,0,0,0] in applycal. A combined solution is stored under the lowest spw id only. Without the map, the default applymode='calflag' passes spws 1–3 through uncorrected and unflagged (only 'calflagstrict' would flag them). Three quarters of the data are not self-calibrated, and no error is raised.
    4. 
    
  4. Why might a weak source "appear" at the phase centre after self-calibrating a blank field?
    AnswerThe solutions fitted the noise to the model, and the corrected data took on the model's phases. It happens when minsnr is too low or the target is too faint for any solint. Estimate the S/N per solution before you start, keep minsnr ≥ 3, and compare with the image made without self-cal.
