1

What ALMA sees in the continuum

Three emission mechanisms with different spectra. Which one you image depends on the band.

Conceptcontinuum emission

Continuum spectra in the ALMA bands

TryTurn all three on. Free-free and synchrotron dominate Band 3, dust above ~150 GHz. Lower β flattens the dust. At 10 K the Rayleigh–Jeans law fails in Bands 6–7 (α < 2+β there) and the spectrum peaks near Band 10. Free-free alone with turnover 200 GHz (a compact H II region) rises as ν² through Band 3, like dust. mfs averages over the in-band slope. nterms=2 measures it.

Conceptcontinuum emission

Continuum emission mechanisms

Thermal dust

  • Grains at 10–100 K in disks, cores and galaxies.
  • Modified blackbody; Rayleigh–Jeans: S ∝ ν2+β. β ≈ 1.5–2 for interstellar grains, ≲ 1 where grains have grown (disks like TW Hya).
  • Brightest in Bands 6–10.

Free-free

  • Thermal bremsstrahlung: electrons passing ions in H II regions, ionised winds and jets.
  • Optically thick S ∝ ν², thin S ∝ ν−0.1. The turnover frequency rises with the emission measure (denser, more compact regions).
  • Strong in Bands 1–4.

Synchrotron

  • Relativistic electrons in magnetic fields: AGN, supernova remnants, star-forming galaxies.
  • S ∝ ν−0.7, steepening as the electrons age. Flat (α ≈ 0) in compact AGN cores.
  • Quasar calibrators are synchrotron sources.

For tclean: specmode='mfs' makes one image of the whole band, as if the spectrum were flat across it. deconvolver='mtmfs', nterms=2 also fits the slope and writes .alpha and .alpha.error (backup slide). To separate mechanisms, image two or more bands at matched resolution and compare.

Set oncespw · line-free channels

Multi-frequency synthesis and line-free channels

  • Multi-frequency synthesis: each channel has its own (u,v) = B/λ, so wide bandwidth also fills in the uv-plane.
  • Exclude line channels with an spw:channel selection, flags, or the pipeline's cont.dat (from hif_findcont). Its ranges are LSRK frequencies. Convert them to channels of your MS, or leave a margin.
  • Average to go faster (split(width=…, timebin=…)). Too much averaging smears sources far from the phase centre (bandwidth and time smearing), more so on long baselines.
  • Check plotms amplitude vs channel for remaining lines.
spw='0:0~395;520~1235;1355~1919'   # 2 lines left out

Set oncecell

Choosing tclean parameters: cell

TrySet cell to 0.4″. Baselines outside ±1/(2·cell) fall off the uv grid and are ignored, so the beam grows. Cells below about beam/5 (≈0.07″ here) do not improve the image and only cost memory and time.

Set onceimsize

Choosing tclean parameters: imsize

TryAt Band 6, a 20 mJy source is 14″ out. With imsize=128 it is off the image, so CLEAN cannot model it and its sidelobes remain in the residual. At 512 (40″, 1.6 × the PB FWHM) it is cleaned and the residual drops to ~1σ.

Set oncepbcor, pblimit

Primary beam correction

TrySix identical 1 mJy sources. Without correction the outer ones look faint. With pbcor=True they are back at 1 mJy, but the noise grows as 1/PB. Measure fluxes on .image.pbcor and noise on .image.

4

Your choice

Parameters you tune for your science: weighting, stopping criteria, masks and deconvolver.

Your choiceweighting, robust

Choosing tclean parameters: weighting

TrySweep robust from 2 to −2. Short baselines get less weight, so the beam shrinks and the noise doubles. The resolved disk also dims (fewer Jy per smaller beam), unlike a point source.

Your choiceniter, nsigma, usemask

Stopping criteria and masks

TryStep niter 0 → 100 → 300, as in First Look. The disk fills in, but niter stops CLEAN with disk still in the residual (rms/σ 1.25). At 20000, nsigma=3 stops it after ≈ 650 iterations, with a noise-like residual. At 1σ with no mask CLEAN models the noise (rms/σ < 1).

Your choiceusemask='auto-multithresh'

Auto-multithresh grows the mask at every major cycle

The threshold is set by the sidelobe term in early cycles (bright peak) and by the noise term in late cycles. Starting values from the CASA automasking guide, by b75 (75% of baselines are shorter; weblog antenna setup page, baselines tab):

Parameter12-m, b75 < 300 m300–400 m> 400 m7-m
sidelobethreshold2.02.02.51.25
noisethreshold4.255.05.05.0
lownoisethreshold1.51.51.52.0
minbeamfrac0.30.30.30.1

tclean's defaults (3.0, 5.0, 1.5, 0.3) match none of these columns, so set all four. Check the .mask image after every run. If it covers noise, raise noisethreshold. If it misses faint emission, lower lownoisethreshold or draw a mask: iclean (CASA ≥ 6.7.2, no viewer on macOS), interactive=True on Linux, or a CRTF region.

Your choicedeconvolver, scales

Multiscale clean for extended emission

TrySame niter, both deconvolvers. Hogbom places point components and leaves a lumpy residual. multiscale fits blobs (pixels: 0 = point, 4 ≈ 1 beam, 12 ≈ 3 beams). Largest scale < MRS.

Checkbefore measuring

Continuum image checklist

Before cleaning

  • Expected rms from the weblog (Part 3) or the sensitivity calculator.
  • Image the phase calibrator (intent CALIBRATE_PHASE, field 3 in TW Hya) with the same call. Expect a point source at the phase centre. If not, check the calibration (Part 3).
  • Look at the PSF: beam size, elongation, sidelobe level.
  • Line channels excluded; cell gives ≥3–5 px per beam; imsize covers the PB.

After cleaning

  • The residual looks like noise, inside and outside the mask.
  • rms (off-source, .image) is close to expected. A large excess likely means calibration or dynamic-range limits (→ Part 6, self-calibration).
  • The .mask covers emission only.

Measuring

  • Fluxes from .image.pbcor (imstat, imfit, CARTA regions).
  • Add the absolute flux-scale uncertainty (≈5–10% at Bands 3–7, more at high frequency).
  • Beam and units from imhead; Jy/beam → K with the Rayleigh–Jeans formula.

Keep

  • The tclean call, in a script.
  • exportfits the products you publish (image, pbcor, pb, residual).
  • Note CASA and pipeline versions.

Choosing cell and imsize from the data

tb = tabletool(); tb.open(VIS)
uvw = tb.getcol('UVW')
flag_row = tb.getcol('FLAG_ROW'); tb.close()
lam = 299792458.0 / 372.65e9                  # wavelength, m
# longest unflagged projected baseline, in λ
umax = np.hypot(uvw[0], uvw[1])[~flag_row].max() / lam
cell = 206265.0 / umax / 5                    # ″, 5 pixels per beam
pb = 6300.0 / 372.65                          # ″, 12-m PB FWHM
imsize = 1.5 * pb / 0.1                       # ≈ 254, use 250
  • Read the longest unflagged projected baseline from the data: the "Optional check of the cell-size rule of thumb" cell prints 368 m = 458 kλ. First Look reads ≈ 429 kλ off an Amp vs UVwave plot of Ceres (field 2), so beam ≈ 0.48″. Cell ≈ 0.1″ in both cases.
  • Coarser cells lose the long baselines. Finer cells only cost time.
  • tclean's log warns when imsize is slow to FFT and suggests a better size.

Your choicedeconvolver='mtmfs', nterms

Wide bandwidths: mtmfs

Plain mfs assumes the sky is the same at every frequency in your band. For a source with spectral index α, that is wrong by (ν/ν₀)α across the band.

  • Matters when the band spans a large fraction of the frequency (Band 3: the two sidebands cover ≈90–106 GHz, ≈16%; Band 1; combined bands) or when the dynamic range is high (≳ 1000).
  • nterms=2 models each component's spectrum as a two-term Taylor series in (ν−ν₀)/ν₀. The spectral index follows as α = I₁/I₀ (dust ≈ 3.5–4 for interstellar dust, ≈ 2–3 in protoplanetary disks; free-free ≈ −0.1 to 2; synchrotron ≈ −0.7).
  • Outputs .image.tt0 (intensity at reffreq), .image.tt1, .alpha and .alpha.error.
  • α is noisy. Use it only where tt0 has high S/N, and check .alpha.error.
tclean(vis='b3_cont.ms', imagename='b3_mt',
       specmode='mfs',
       deconvolver='mtmfs', nterms=2,
       reffreq='100GHz',
       scales=[0, 5, 15],  # optional: multi-term multi-scale
       imsize=512, cell='0.1arcsec',
       weighting='briggs', robust=0.5,
       niter=20000, nsigma=2.5,
       usemask='auto-multithresh', pbcor=True)

Checkquestions

Four questions before the hands-on session

  1. Your beam is 0.30″ × 0.25″ in Band 6. Pick cell and imsize for a single pointing.
    AnswerAbout 0.05″ (5 pixels across the minor axis). The PB FWHM is ≈25″, and 1.5 × 25″ / 0.05″ = 750 pixels, so imsize=750, which is a fast FFT size (2·3·5³; check with synthesisutils().getOptimumSize(750)).
  2. Going from robust=2 to robust=-1 shrinks your beam by about a third. What happens to the noise, and why?
    AnswerIt rises (about ×2 in the weighting demo, typically ×1.5–2.5). The many short baselines that carry most of the sensitivity are down-weighted, so fewer visibilities effectively contribute.
  3. hif_makeimages reports a continuum rms 2.5× the expected value (Part 3). What do you look at before imaging?
    AnswerThe earlier stages: QA messages, flagging, and the phase-vs-time plots of hifa_timegaincal. Noisy phases or a bright source may limit the dynamic range. Self-calibration may help (Part 6). Deeper cleaning will not. Also check that the continuum ranges are line-free.
  4. After niter=100000 without a mask the residual looks flat and the disk flux changed. What happened?
    AnswerCLEAN modelled the noise. The residual rms is artificially low and the model contains noise components. Clean inside a mask (or auto-multithresh) down to ≈2–3σ.
