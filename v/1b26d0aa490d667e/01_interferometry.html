1

What an interferometer measures

Fringes, the uv-plane and the dirty image.

Conceptone baseline · the interference pattern

One baseline: the fringe pattern

One visibility (amplitude, phase) = the sum of the sky weighted by fringes λ/B apart.

TryGauss 2″, 15 m → 1 km. |V| falls as λ/B nears the source size (0.15 at 100 m), ≈ 0 once two fringes fit. Point 1″ east has |V| = 1, only the phase changes. The angle rotates the fringes.

Conceptall baselines · van Cittert–Zernike

The visibilities are the Fourier transform of the sky

V(u,v) = ∬ A(l,m) I(l,m) e−2πi(ul+vm) dl dm (van Cittert–Zernike). Each baseline is one point (u, v) = B/λ. A baseline B at wavelength λ responds to structure on scales of ≈ λ/B.

TryGrow the Gaussian from 0.1″ to 4″. Its visibilities move to the centre of the uv-plane, where the array has no baselines. On the sky it fades (the same 1 Jy, spread thinner).

Fixed by the observationuv coverage · the PSF

The array samples the uv-plane only at discrete points

TryCompare 2 min with 4 h, then C-2 with C-6. Earth rotation turns each baseline into a track. The ≈15 m central hole is too small to see here (next slide).

Fixed by the observationmissing short spacings · maximum recoverable scale

Missing short spacings and the maximum recoverable scale

TryMake the source bigger than the MRS. Flux is lost and a negative bowl appears. This is missing short spacings, not a ring. No imaging parameter recovers it. The 7-m array (ACA) recovers part of the flux, Total Power the rest.

Conceptgaps in the uv-plane · why we deconvolve

Gaps in uv coverage: why we clean

Dirty image = true sky ⊛ dirty beam (PSF). Gaps in the uv-plane produce PSF sidelobes around every source.

Try16 antennas, 2 min gives large sidelobes, stripes and holes. CLEAN (Part 4) removes most of them using the PSF. 43 antennas, 4 h gives smaller sidelobes. CLEAN cannot recover unmeasured data.

2

What else the observation fixes

Field of view, sensitivity, and the parameters you choose.

Fixed by the observationfield of view · the primary beam

Field of view: the primary beam

TryGo from Band 3 to Band 9. The field shrinks from about 60″ to under 10″ (FWHM ≈ 1.13 λ/D). Sources away from the centre appear fainter because the primary beam response is lower there. Larger areas need a mosaic of pointings.

Fixed by the observationsensitivity · the radiometer equation

Sensitivity: the radiometer equation

σ ≈ 2kTsys / ( η Aeff √(N(N−1) npol Δν t) )

Try4× the time on source halves the noise (σ ∝ 1/√t). Halving the beam at fixed rms raises the brightness-temperature noise 4×. Your images should reach roughly this rms. The weblog lists the same theoretical value.

Rules of thumb

Approximate values from the simulated 43-antenna layouts. Real numbers: ALMA Technical Handbook and the Sensitivity Calculator.

Dirty image = sky ⊛ PSF

Stripes and negative holes before CLEAN are normal (Part 4). Artifacts after CLEAN may point to a data problem (Part 3).

Resolution

θ ≈ 0.574 λ / L80 (80th-percentile baseline).

Maximum recoverable scale

θMRS ≈ 0.983 λ / L5. Larger structures are resolved out. No imaging parameter recovers them.

Field of view

Primary beam FWHM ≈ 1.13 λ / D (≈ 25″ at 230 GHz, 12-m dishes).

Noise

σ ∝ Tsys / √(N(N−1) Δν t). 4× the time on source halves σ.

Checkdiscussion

Four questions

  1. Your 6″ cloud was observed only in C-4 at Band 6. Which tclean parameter recovers its smooth emission?
    AnswerNone. It is larger than the MRS (≈5″), so these scales were not measured. You need ACA (and Total Power) data or a more compact configuration.
  2. Why does the same configuration give a smaller beam at Band 7 than at Band 3?
    AnswerResolution scales with λ/B. The same baselines are 3.5× longer in wavelengths at 345 GHz than at 100 GHz.
  3. You need half the noise of an existing 1-hour observation. How long must the new one be?
    AnswerFour hours (σ ∝ 1/√t), with the same setup and similar weather.
  4. Your dirty image shows negative holes and stripes around a bright source. Are the data bad?
    AnswerNot necessarily. The dirty image is the sky convolved with the dirty beam, sidelobes included. Deconvolve first (Part 4). Artifacts left after CLEAN may point to a data problem (Part 3, weblogs).
